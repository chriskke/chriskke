# Hey, I'm Chris

I'll try pretty much anything at least once. Sometimes that's a new framework, sometimes it's fixing a server at 2am. Results vary.

Right now I'm an **AI Engineer** at [Calibrax AI](https://www.calibrax.ai/), a software consultancy in Malaysia.

### How I got here

I graduated from **XMUM (Xiamen University Malaysia)** in 2023 with a degree in **Digital Media Technology Engineering**. It's basically half creative, half code, and I still use both halves every day.

I didn't jump straight into coding after graduating. From 2023 to 2024 I did marketing, shot and edited videos, worked on SEO, and built analytics systems.

What hooked me wasn't one tool or one job. It was the feeling of finding a real problem and actually fixing it. So I started teaching myself by building apps, breaking them, and building them again. No bootcamp, just a lot of trial, error and Googling.

That's pretty much how I still learn today.

### What I do now

- **AI engineering:** building AI-powered tools and automation for real business problems
- **Web apps:** mostly JavaScript and TypeScript
- **Infra and DevOps:** Kubernetes, AWS, autoscaling (so things don't fall over when real traffic shows up)
- **Data problems:** collecting, cleaning and making sense of messy data
- **Still a creative at heart:** I care how things look and feel, not just whether they run

I know my way around a lot of the stack, but every project shows me there's more to learn. Honestly, that's the fun part.

### My take on AI

Things are moving crazy fast. I'd be lying if I said I don't use AI coding agents. I do, every day. It's literally my job.

But there's a line between **using AI as a tool** and **letting it do all the thinking**. I try to stay on the right side of it. AI makes me faster, but I still want to know what it wrote and why.

Here's what a lot of vibe coders miss: getting an app working on your laptop is easy now. Keeping it up when 10,000 people hit it at once is a different story. That's the part I enjoy.
